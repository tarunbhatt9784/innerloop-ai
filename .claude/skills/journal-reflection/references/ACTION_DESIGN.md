# Action Design

## Optimize for probability of execution

A good experiment is small enough to start, specific enough to know whether it happened, and useful even when it does not produce the hoped-for emotional result.

## Preferred structure

`When [cue], I will [observable action] for [small dose]. I count it as a win when [finish line]. If resistance is high, I will do [minimum version]. Afterward I will notice [signal].`

## Friction reducers

Use only those relevant to the action:

- pre-position needed objects;
- reduce duration;
- attach to an existing routine;
- choose an exact time/place;
- remove a decision at execution time;
- prepare a one-sentence script for interpersonal actions;
- define the first 30 seconds;
- create a fallback that still counts as showing up.

## Avoid

- vague goals such as "be positive";
- multiple habits bundled together;
- actions dependent on another person's response;
- large life decisions based on one journal entry;
- punishment, deprivation, sleep restriction, unsafe exercise, or other coercive self-management.
