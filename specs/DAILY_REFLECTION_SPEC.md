# Daily Reflection Spec

## Goal

Choose one next behavioural experiment that is psychologically plausible, strongly grounded in the current journal, useful for learning, and highly likely to be completed.

## Interpretation discipline

Maintain four layers:

1. **Observation** — directly supported by journal evidence.
2. **Pattern** — repeated observation across multiple entries or strongly corroborated current evidence.
3. **Hypothesis** — possible mechanism that explains observations.
4. **Fact** — only objective, well-supported information; never promote a psychological hypothesis to fact.

## Candidate generation

Generate 2-5 candidate experiments internally. Consider evidence-informed approaches such as behavioural activation, implementation intentions, stimulus control, problem decomposition, values-guided action, cognitive distancing, attention management, self-compassionate reappraisal, graded exposure to ordinary avoided tasks, communication experiments, and recovery routines.

Do not use exposure-style suggestions for trauma, severe phobia, self-harm, dangerous situations, or clinically complex conditions without appropriate professional involvement.

## Action scoring

Score each candidate from 0-5 on:

- `relevance`: directly targets today's strongest actionable pattern;
- `controllability`: can be done mostly by the user without another person's cooperation;
- `friction`: low time, effort, setup, and emotional cost (higher score = lower friction);
- `specificity`: clear cue, behaviour, duration, and finish line;
- `reversibility`: safe to stop or modify;
- `learning_value`: result will reveal something useful even if outcome is neutral;
- `fit`: consistent with known preferences, constraints, and prior experiment history.

Weight completion probability more heavily than theoretical impact.

Reject any candidate that conflicts with `SAFETY_SPEC.md`.

## Minimum viable win

The selected experiment must define:

- **Cue**: when/where it happens;
- **Action**: one observable behaviour;
- **Dose**: smallest useful duration or quantity;
- **Win condition**: binary or clearly observable;
- **Fallback**: a smaller version if resistance is high;
- **Review signal**: what to notice afterward.

The fallback should normally take under two minutes unless the task itself cannot meaningfully be reduced.

## One-change invariant

Return one primary experiment only. Do not hide multiple behaviour changes inside one recommendation.
